# Research presentation

Speaker: LIANG ZIHAN. Slides 12–14; about two minutes including the close. Method: authored interview scenarios and generated task/SUS responses, with no recruitment or observation.

## Slide 12 — simulated interviews and usability study

This is a simulated study: we authored six student and three organiser interview scenarios and generated one study of twelve participant profiles; no people were recruited or observed. Two scripted pre-study walkthroughs are excluded. Eight profiles get participant tasks and four organiser tasks. Each uses both SeatRelay and prepared per-session forms with a spreadsheet, in counterbalanced order and equivalent A/B datasets.

The generated study has 112 task attempts and 24 ten-item SUS forms. Unassisted completion is 89.3 percent for SeatRelay versus 78.6 for the control. Mean SUS is 69.4 versus 53.3. The median individual paired time reduction is 38.7 percent. Timing is paired by person; repeated tasks are not independent people.

The scripts highlight held-versus-confirmed confusion, manual cancellation work and missed deadlines. Counterexamples remain: simple forms can suffice, fifteen minutes may be too short, and procurement may need approval. Completion and SUS miss their reference targets. We recommend clearer state and deadline wording, ranking guidance and date-entry help; these changes have not been evaluated in this study.

## Slide 13 — limitations and roles

One writer per workshop preserves order at a throughput cost. Deployment is single-region. The interviews, the 12-person study and the live pilot promised in the Preliminary report were not held with people; the pilot slot lapsed and is to be rebooked.  Research outcomes are generated from declared assumptions, so they demonstrate the method rather than establish real user benefit. Existing deployed system tests remain separate evidence. The report states the assistance used and each member's role.
