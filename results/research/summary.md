# SeatRelay interviews and user research (simulated)

This report contains nine authored interview profiles and generated task/SUS responses for twelve simulated participant profiles (no people were recruited or observed), covering the research design, analysis and design implications.

## 1. Research questions and study design

The scope is one scripted study with two tool conditions per participant profile. Two pre-study walkthrough scenarios are excluded from the formal sample. The research questions concern registration effort, manual coordination after cancellation, interpretation of held versus confirmed seats, deadlines and queue rules.

Interview scenarios cover 6 students and 3 organisers. The study has 12 participants: 8 complete participant tasks T1-T5 and 4 with organising experience complete O1-O4. Each uses SeatRelay (SR) and the control (CTRL).

The control is prepared per-session forms plus a shared spreadsheet with duplicate-detection formulas. The original counterbalance.csv assigns order and equivalent A/B datasets: six use each tool first; the organiser track has two in each order. Dataset assignments are balanced within every task and condition.

There are 112 task attempts and 24 ten-item SUS forms. Completion uses task attempts as its denominator. Timing first sums each person's tasks by condition and then compares 12 paired totals; repeated tasks are not counted as independent participants. The task cap is 300 seconds; outcomes distinguish unassisted success, assisted success and failure.

## 2. Data sources and generation method

Interview scenarios were authored and task/questionnaire responses generated by a reproducible model. No people were recruited or observed. The data demonstrate an evaluation workflow and design trade-offs. Raw tables retain source_type=SIMULATED; they do not establish empirical usability, adoption or willingness to pay.

The fixed seed is 20261031; it was not searched for favourable outcomes. Profiles, adverse events and baseline times are declared in tools/research/scenarios.json. Shared person-level and task-level timing factors use log-normal multipliers. The second tool condition receives a 0.94 practice multiplier; dataset B receives a 1.04 difficulty multiplier. T3 and O3 deliberately allow the prepared spreadsheet to be faster.

Authored failure and help events remain in the dataset; an assisted attempt that reaches the cap is recorded as a timeout (one CTRL case). Organiser base action counts use the upper end of each protocol range. An incorrect final answer may fail before 300 seconds; timeout events reach the cap. Failed attempts retain their recorded time in each person's total. SUS starts with ten integer responses from 1 to 5, then uses odd/even reverse scoring to compute the total.

Statements are scripts rather than transcripts. There are no interview dates, recordings, recruitment or consent forms. scenario_date identifies the scenario version; consent=not_applicable_simulated. One author codes complete profile scripts; no thematic saturation, independent double coding or intercoder agreement is claimed.

## 3. Interview findings

| Theme | Profiles covered / 9 | Traceable profiles | Design implication |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Unclear state and next action | 6 | SIM-I01, SIM-I02, SIM-I04, SIM-I05, SIM-I06, SIM-I08 | State the current status, required action and consequence of inaction together |
| Duplicate registration as insurance | 3 | SIM-I01, SIM-I05, SIM-I07 | Collect acceptable sessions and preferences once; explain the one-seat-per-workshop rule |
| Manual coordination after cancellation | 5 | SIM-I03, SIM-I06, SIM-I07, SIM-I08, SIM-I09 | Automate backfill with an explanatory log; distinguish an offer from its acceptance |
| Confirmation windows and notification reach | 5 | SIM-I01, SIM-I02, SIM-I03, SIM-I06, SIM-I09 | Allow organisers to configure holds and display the deadline clearly |
| Queue fairness and explainability | 5 | SIM-I02, SIM-I03, SIM-I05, SIM-I07, SIM-I09 | Explain both arrival order and acceptable-session constraints; avoid promising an exact wait |
| Permissions, data and procurement constraints | 4 | SIM-I04, SIM-I07, SIM-I08, SIM-I09 | Provide CSV export and organisation permissions; validate pricing and procurement separately |

Theme frequency describes coverage of these nine scripts, not population prevalence. Full records are interviews/SIM-I01.md through SIM-I09.md; coded-excerpts.csv links statements to profile-level codes.

Counterexamples are retained: SIM-I04 finds a simple form sufficient; SIM-I03 cannot check email within fifteen minutes; SIM-I09 lacks procurement authority even if the features seem valuable. These cases expose login overhead, context-dependent confirmation windows and institutional data/procurement constraints.

## 4. Comparative study results

| Metric | SeatRelay (SR) | Forms + sheet (CTRL) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Unassisted success / attempts | 50/56 (89.3%) | 44/56 (78.6%) |
| Assisted successes / failures | 4 / 2 | 7 / 5 |
| Completion including assistance | 96.4% | 91.1% |
| Median total task time per participant (seconds) | 322.5 | 384.5 |
| SUS mean / median (0-100) | 69.4 / 68.75 | 53.3 / 51.25 |
| Total errors / help requests | 15 / 8 | 24 / 14 |
| Median manual actions per organiser (four tasks) | 13.5 | 28.0 |

The median individual paired time reduction is **38.7%**; SR is faster for **9/12** participants. The calculation is the median of each person's 100 x (CTRL total - SR total) / CTRL total, rather than the ratio of the two condition medians. Tracks are reported separately in summary.json because four-task and five-task totals are not comparable workloads.

| Track | SR unassisted completion | CTRL unassisted completion |
|---|---:|---:|
| Participant | 36/40 (90.0%) | 32/40 (80.0%) |
| Organiser | 14/16 (87.5%) | 12/16 (75.0%) |

Against the original reference targets: unassisted completion >=90% is not met at 89.3%; paired time reduction >=25% is met at 38.7%; mean SUS >=75 is not met at 69.4. These are comparisons of model outputs with reference lines, not empirical acceptance verdicts.

No statistical significance or population confidence interval is claimed for generated responses. SUS is a usability scale score, not a satisfaction percentage. The 112 task rows do not represent 112 independent participants.

## 5. Negative cases and design priorities

| Priority | Traceable issue | Recommendation and future evaluation criterion |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | SIM-P08/T3 and SIM-P11/T4: held interpreted as confirmed | Show current state, acceptance deadline and next action together; assess state interpretation and confirmation without hints |
| P1 | SIM-P02/T1 and SIM-P06/O1: ranking and date entry need help | Add contextual guidance; assess discoverability and date-format errors in both condition periods |
| P1 | SIM-I03 and SIM-P07: short holds conflict with offline periods | Keep configurable duration and disclose it before registration; compare appropriate 15/30/60-minute windows by context |
| P1 | SIM-P09/O3 and SIM-I05: queue counts lack a clear definition | Explain acceptable-session constraints and queue order; separate confirmed, held, cumulative registrations and attendance |
| P2 | SIM-I04 and SIM-P04: simple events may not benefit | Include a no-waitlist event in future evaluation and assess login/confirmation overhead |

These are recommendations derived from this analysis. The corresponding interface changes have not been implemented or evaluated in this study.

## 6. Pricing and market scenarios

The three organiser scenarios specify annual and multi-session event counts, yielding an event-weighted multi-session share of **37.0%**. Median baseline coordination is **4.0 hours/event**. Assumed bargain and expensive monthly prices have medians of **S$8** and **S$15**. Student prices are per event; organiser prices are per organisation-month and are never pooled with student prices.

Baseline coordination is not time saved and cannot be reported as an adoption benefit. Two of three organiser scenarios permit independent tool selection; a separate 10% adoption share is a planning assumption. These inputs illustrate TAM/SAM/SOM formulas; they do not establish observed demand, purchasing commitments or a representative market share. Three scripts cannot estimate a validated price curve.

## 7. Timing-model sensitivity

| SR baseline-time multiplier | Median paired reduction | Participants faster with SR |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.80 | 51.0% | 11/12 |
| 1.00 | 38.7% | 9/12 |
| 1.20 | 26.8% | 9/12 |

Only the SR timing baseline changes. The seed, authored events and other assumptions remain fixed. These are scenario comparisons, not confidence intervals, showing how the timing conclusion depends on the assumed interaction cost.

## 8. Interpretation and deliverable scope

H1: the scenario illustrates how unified registration could reduce effort, while exposing state misinterpretation and login overhead; actual user benefit remains unmeasured. H3: cancellation/export workflows reduce manual actions in the model, but real organiser time savings have not been measured. H2 engine simulations and existing deployed-system experiments are separate evidence.

The interview records, coding, comparative data, SUS calculations, charts, sensitivity analysis and report are complete. Subsequent field research should use an independent directory, retain the provenance of this package and recompute conclusions from the new observations.
