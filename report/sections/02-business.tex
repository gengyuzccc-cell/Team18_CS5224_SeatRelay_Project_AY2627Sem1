\section{Business model and cost}\label{sec:business}

\paragraph{Customers, pricing and value.}
Organisers pay; participants never pay or install anything. We propose two plans: a free tier with
one open workshop at a time, and an organiser plan at \sd10 per organisation per month (\sd120 a
year) with any number of open workshops and co-organisers. The prototype does not enforce plan
limits. The first channel is NUS student
organisations and units that run repeated workshops, starting with the organiser of the planned
pilot (\S\ref{sec:pilot}). The price is defensible if
$h \cdot w \cdot k \geq P$, where $h$ is the coordination time saved per multi-session event, $w$
the value of an organiser hour and $k$ the number of such events per organiser-year. The organiser
interviews give $h$ (median \InterviewHoursMedian{} hours per event) and the willingness to pay
(median price felt to be a bargain \InterviewWtpBargainMedian{}, getting expensive
\InterviewWtpExpensiveMedian{} \sd/month). The simulation in \S\ref{sec:mechanism} gives one
component of $h$: with per-session forms, \ReplayDupConfirmedPerSessionMean{} confirmed seats per
workshop end as duplicates that the organiser must chase, against \ReplayDupConfirmedUnifiedMean{}
with one queue.

\paragraph{Cost accounting in three layers.}
Cloud cost is computed as usage $\times$ unit price for the \CostDimensionsUsed{} billable dimensions
the stacks use in \code{ap-southeast-1}; \CostPricesVerified{} of the \CostPricesTotal{} entries of
the price file (which also holds two switched-off alternatives) were read from the AWS Price List API
on \CostPricingDate~\cite{aws-pricelist-bulk-api}; the main ones are on the API Gateway, Lambda and
DynamoDB pricing pages~\cite{aws-pricing-apigw,aws-pricing-lambda,aws-pricing-dynamodb}. The layers are (1)~list
price, (2)~after the always-free allowances~\cite{aws-free-tier}, and (3)~after credits, which is
\usd0 while the account's credits last. Layer~3 is why billing tools show a net cost of zero; our
budget alarms therefore exclude credits, whose inclusion is the default~\cite{aws-budgets-costtypes}. Budget
data are updated only up to three times a day~\cite{aws-budgets}, so, as planned, throttling, log
retention and teardown, not the alarms, bound spend.
Until measured usage is priced (\CostMeasuredListUsd), usage per registration is modelled from the
design: \CostAssumeCommandsPerRegistration{} commands and \CostAssumeOffersPerRegistration{} offers
per registration, \CostAssumeReadsPerCommand{} API reads per command including polling, and
\CostAssumeMauPerRegistration{} Cognito monthly active users per registration (all assumptions are
listed in \code{results/cost/model-summary.md}).

\paragraph{On-premise reference.}
The same engine and single-page application (SPA) also run on a self-hosted stack
(\code{services/local}: Docker Compose with PostgreSQL, Keycloak, Mailpit and nginx). That compose
file is the functional reference; the priced service would run it on one mini-PC with Keycloak in
production mode behind TLS, a transactional e-mail provider instead of the Mailpit sink, and backups.
\IfPending{\PerfLocalCpuPeakPct}{We have not load-tested it
(\pending{k6 and docker-stats run of services/local}), so}{Under the k6 matrix it peaked at
\PerfLocalCpuPeakPct\,\% of one core and \PerfLocalMemPeakMiB\,MiB;} one machine's capacity
(\CostOnpremCapacityRegs{} registrations a month) is an assumption. Two views are
priced. \emph{Increment only}: an N100 mini-PC at \sd\CostOnpremServerPriceSgd~\cite{onprem-newegg}
with a UPS~\cite{onprem-ups} and a backup disk~\cite{onprem-disk}, amortised over
\CostAmortisationYears{} years; \CostOnpremLoadWatts\,W at load~\cite{onprem-notebookcheck} at
\CostOnpremTariffCents{} cents/kWh~\cite{onprem-sp-tariff}; a static IP~\cite{onprem-static-ip};
e-mail at the SES price; no labour. \emph{Fully amortised}: plus a business fibre line at
\sd\CostOnpremLineSgd{} a month~\cite{onprem-m1-fibre}, an off-site backup copy~\cite{onprem-b2} and
operations labour of \CostOnpremLabourHours{}\,h a month at \sd\CostOnpremLabourRateSgd/h, which is
our assumption; \sd\CostFxSgdPerUsd{} = \usd1~\cite{onprem-fx}. Load, retention and function
are held equal in the model; availability is not: one machine has no failover, whereas the cloud
services span several availability zones (AZs).

\paragraph{Result.}
At 1{,}000 registrations a month the modelled cloud list price (\usd\CostTierOneKCloudListUsd) is
mostly CloudWatch's fixed items, a dashboard, custom metrics and alarms
(\usd\CostSvcCloudWatchOneKListUsd), and Cognito monthly active users
(\usd\CostSvcCognitoUserPoolsOneKListUsd), which the free allowances cover; what remains is
\usd\CostTierOneKCloudAfterFreeUsd{} (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). At 100{,}000 registrations Cognito
dominates the list price (\usd\CostSvcCognitoUserPoolsHundredKListUsd{} of
\usd\CostTierHundredKCloudListUsd). The fully amortised on-prem view is nearly flat because labour
(\usd\CostOnpremOneKFullLabourUsd) and the business line (\usd\CostOnpremOneKFullNetworkUsd) dominate
it. The cloud list price exceeds the increment-only view above \CostCrossoverListIncrementRegs{}
registrations a month and the fully amortised view above \CostCrossoverListFullRegs{}
(Table~\ref{tab:cost}, right; after the free tier: \CostCrossoverAfterFreeIncrementRegs{} and
\CostCrossoverAfterFreeFullRegs). Below the first crossover the cloud build is the cheaper option in
both views; up to the second it is cheaper than an on-prem service that pays for its own line and
operations. It costs nothing out of pocket while credits last and buys multi-AZ availability, which
a single volunteer-run machine gives up.
