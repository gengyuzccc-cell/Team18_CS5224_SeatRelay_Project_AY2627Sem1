\section{Motivation and business case}\label{sec:motivation}

\paragraph{Problem.}
Many workshops on campus and in continuing education and training (CET) run the same content in
several sessions so that people with different timetables can attend. Registration is usually one
form per session plus a spreadsheet. A participant who could attend two sessions registers for
both to be safe; the organiser later has to find and release the duplicate seat by hand, and a seat
freed by a dropout stays empty unless someone e-mails the next person on a waiting list. Duplicate
holds block seats that other people want, and the reconciliation is manual work for the organiser.

\paragraph{SeatRelay.}
A participant registers \emph{once} per workshop with an ordered list of acceptable sessions and
joins \emph{one} queue; an account holds at most one seat per workshop (Fig.~\ref{fig:flow}). A free
seat is offered to the first queued person who accepts that session and held for \code{holdSeconds}
(default 900\,s); a seat released by an expiry, a decline, a cancellation or a capacity increase goes
to the next person in the same decision. Organisers manage 2--5 sessions and see per-session seats,
the queue, a backfill log with latencies, distinct participants served, attendance and a CSV export;
participants see their status and queue position and get in-app and e-mail notifications.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.}
As in the Preliminary report, these are stated as hypotheses, not findings. (H1)~unified
registration reduces the participant effort needed to register and track a waitlist; (H2)~the
cross-session constraint removes the capacity waste caused by one account holding seats in several
sessions at once; (H3)~automatic backfill reduces the organiser's manual handling of cancellations
and offers. We make no claim about attendance rates, satisfaction or paying customers
(verdicts: \S\ref{sec:hypotheses}).

\paragraph{Evidence that repeated sessions occur.}
On the NUS Libraries calendar from \MarketLibcalFrom{} to \MarketLibcalTo, \MarketNusLibcalRepeatEvents{}
of the \MarketNusLibcalWorkshops{} events tagged Workshop were repeats of \MarketNusLibcalRepeatTitles{}
titles (a library tour \MarketNusLibcalRepeatTitleOneEvents{} times, an orientation workshop
\MarketNusLibcalRepeatTitleTwoEvents{} times)~\cite{nus-libcal}; on the SMU Libraries calendar in the
same window, \MarketSmuLibcalRepeatTitles{} titles ran twice among \MarketSmuLibcalEvents{}
events~\cite{smu-libcal}. These are our script's counts of the public calendars
(\code{results/market/}). Most titles ran once, so calendars show that the situation exists but not
how often an organiser faces it; that share, $p$, must come from organiser interviews.

\paragraph{Market size.}
We size the market bottom-up (Table~\ref{tab:market}): the number of organisers who could run
multi-session sign-ups, times $p$, times the price $P$ of \sd120 per organiser-year
(\S\ref{sec:business}). Singapore has six autonomous universities and five
polytechnics~\cite{moe-au,moe-psei}; five of the universities list at least \MarketStudentOrgs{}
student organisations, a lower bound because counts given as \enquote{more than $X$} enter as $X$
and the sixth university (SUSS) and the polytechnics are not counted (\MarketStudentOrgsFetched{} if
only pages we could download and our own counts are used). With $p = \text{\InterviewPEventWeightedPct}\,\%$
(event-weighted share of organisers' events with two or more equivalent sessions;
\InterviewOrganisers{} organisers interviewed), the campus total addressable market is
$\text{TAM} = \text{\MarketStudentOrgs} \times p \times \text{\sd\MarketPriceSgd} \approx
\text{\sd\MarketTamCampusSgd}\,p$ per year, and about \sd\MarketTamCampusCetSgd\,$p$ if the more
than \MarketCetProviders{} CET training providers~\cite{ssg-credit-progress-2016} are included. The
serviceable market is $\text{SAM} = s \cdot \text{TAM}$, with $s$ the share of organisers not bound to
a closed institutional system; the first-year obtainable market, NUS student organisations at
adoption share $a$, is $\text{SOM} = \text{\MarketNusOrgs} \times p \times a \times
\text{\sd\MarketPriceSgd} \leq \text{\sd\MarketSomNusMaxSgd}$ a year (\pending{$s$ from the organiser
interviews; the team's assumed adoption share $a$}). Even at $p = 1$ the counted campus market is only about \sd\MarketTamCampusSgd{} a year (more with SUSS
and the polytechnics, but of the same order), so the business case rests on the time each organiser
saves (\S\ref{sec:business}), not on market size.

\paragraph{Competitors.}
Table~\ref{tab:competitors} compares the services of the Preliminary report's Table~1 (Luma,
Eventbrite, Humanitix, per-session forms plus a spreadsheet) and two more, from vendor documentation.
Eventbrite relays a released ticket automatically with a claim window. Humanitix e-mails an offer to
the first waitlisted guest and then to the next every 30 minutes while tickets remain; the interval
cannot be changed and \enquote{tickets are not reserved}~\cite{humanitix-waitlist}, so the
Preliminary report's \enquote{host-configured offer time limit} is not supported by that page and is
withdrawn. In every tool the waitlist belongs to one date, ticket type or slot; none ties several
acceptable sessions to one queue position with one seat per person, which is what removes duplicate
holds.
